Start with the allegation. What specific conduct, date and time are alleged? Which evidence and policy provision are relied on? What consequences are possible? Distinguish an allegation, an employee account and evidence actually produced.
SUGGESTED RESPONSE · NEW EVIDENCE

Request time to respond

“I would like reasonable time to review this new material before giving a complete response. Please record my request, the preparation I need, and the decision made.”

Explain what you need to check. Record the request, time, decision and reasons. This is not an unconditional entitlement to adjourn.

SUGGESTED QUESTION

Clarify classification

“Which exact provision and established facts support gross misconduct in these circumstances?”

Give a truthful account of the conduct you accept or dispute. Classification and sanction require separate consideration.

Official guidance and a closing checklist

LEGAL / ACAS · SUMMARY

Preparation and a fair hearing

The Acas Code says written allegations and possible consequences should be given; normally include written evidence and statements. Allow reasonable preparation and an opportunity to answer, question and present evidence.

Acas Code, paragraphs 9–12 · checked 7 October 2026

OFFICIAL LAW · SUMMARY

Eligible companion

A reasonable request at a qualifying disciplinary hearing gives a right to one eligible companion: another worker of the employer or an eligible trade union official. The companion can put and sum up the case, respond to views and confer; the employer need not let them answer questions for the worker.

If the chosen companion is unavailable, propose a reasonable alternative within the five-working-day statutory period after the original hearing date. There is no automatic entitlement to a solicitor.

Employment Relations Act 1999, section 10 · checked 7 October 2026

PRIVATE PREPARATION CHECKLIST · NO FACTS ASSUMED

Only rely on mitigation you can support

  • Amount, timing and authorisation
  • Actual duties and shift position
  • Evidence about impairment, performance, people and safety
  • Relevant practice and communicated rules
  • Record, contribution and current warnings
  • Acknowledgement and steps to prevent recurrence, where appropriate

Absence of an actual incident does not by itself rule out potential risk or gross misconduct.

SUGGESTED RESPONSE

Before the meeting ends

“Please consider my account, the evidence and the mitigation I can substantiate. Please explain the findings, reasons, outcome and appeal process in writing.”

  • Record unresolved facts and requested evidence.
  • Ask how any corrections to meeting notes can be recorded.
  • Preserve the written decision and original evidence.
  • Confirm appeal recipient and deadline.
  • Consider independent advice; service and circumstances affect available claims.
