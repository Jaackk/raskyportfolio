VAT on Etsy seller fees in the UK: who pays, and what it costs you

Updated 3 November 2026. Figures checked against Etsy's own pages.

There are two different VATs in play for an Etsy seller, and mixing them up is the commonest mistake. One is the VAT Etsy adds to its own fees. The other is the VAT you charge your customers if you are VAT registered.

Quick answer. Etsy says it may apply VAT to its seller fees depending on your business status and location. For UK sellers that is 20% on top of Etsy's fees. If you are not VAT registered it is a cost you cannot reclaim. If you are VAT registered you can normally reclaim it. Separately, if you are VAT registered and your prices include VAT, a sixth of each sale goes to HMRC.

1. VAT on Etsy's fees

Etsy's fee policy says its published fees exclude VAT and that VAT is collected on all Etsy fees for sellers in countries where VAT applies. That includes the transaction fee, payment processing, the regulatory operating fee, listing fees, Offsite Ads and Etsy Ads. This calculator leaves the currency conversion fee out of the VAT base because Etsy does not say it is VATable.

On a £25.00 order with the default fees, the fees before VAT come to £3.10 and VAT adds £0.62.

SellerFees on the orderVAT on fees you bear
Not VAT registered£3.71£0.62
VAT registered (reclaims VAT)£3.10£0.00

2. VAT on your own sales (VAT-registered sellers)

If you are VAT registered, you must account for VAT on your sales. How it affects you depends on how you price:

  • Your listed prices include VAT. A sixth of each sale (20/120) is VAT you owe HMRC. On the £25.00 example order that is £4.17. This comes out of your profit.
  • You add VAT on top. Then the buyer pays the VAT separately and your take is unchanged. This calculator does not model that case.

If you are not VAT registered you do not charge or pay VAT on your sales, so only the first kind of VAT matters.

Do I need to register for VAT?

That depends on your taxable turnover and the current threshold, which HMRC sets and can change. Check GOV.UK for the current figure and talk to an accountant if you are close to it. Etsy also reports UK seller information to HMRC, which is worth knowing when you think about your tax position.
