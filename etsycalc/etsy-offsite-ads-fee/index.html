Etsy Offsite Ads fee: 15% or 12%, the $100 cap and how it hits profit

Updated 3 November 2026. Figures checked against Etsy's own pages.

Offsite Ads is the Etsy fee that can change a good sale into a thin one. It is not charged on every order. It is charged only when Etsy's own advertising brings the buyer to your shop.

Quick answer. Etsy charges 15% of the order total on sales it attributes to Offsite Ads, or 12% once your shop has passed 10,000 USD of sales in the previous 365 days. The fee on one order never exceeds 100 USD. Shops under 10,000 USD can opt out.

When the fee applies

Etsy buys advertising on other sites and platforms. If your listing appears in one of those adverts, a buyer clicks it, and the buyer then places any order from your shop within 30 days, that order is an attributed order and the fee applies. If the buyer's last click before ordering was on an Etsy Ad, only the Etsy Ads fee applies instead. Orders that did not come from an Etsy-placed advert are not charged this fee.

15% or 12%

Which rate you pay depends on your shop's sales over the previous 365 days, calculated on the first day of each month. Under 10,000 USD you pay 15%. Once you reach 10,000 USD or more, Etsy says you pay 12% on attributed orders for the lifetime of your shop, even if sales later fall below the threshold. The threshold counts item price multiplied by quantity, minus discounts and cancelled orders, and excludes postage and taxes.

What it is charged on

The fee is a percentage of the total order amount: the price you display plus postage and gift wrapping, and personalisation if you offer it. Outside the US it also applies to any tax included in your listing prices.

The 100 USD cap

The total Offsite Ads fee on a single attributed order is capped at 100 USD. At 15% the cap starts to bite on orders above roughly 667 USD. In pounds the cap moves with the exchange rate. With the calculator's default rate of 0.75 it is £75.00. A £600.00 order therefore pays the capped £75.00 at 15%.

Opting out

Etsy says shops under 10,000 USD of sales in the last 365 days can opt out of Offsite Ads for as long as they stay under that threshold. Once you cross it, participation is required. Whether to opt out is a business decision: the ads can bring buyers you would not otherwise reach, but every attributed sale costs 15%. The calculator lets you compare both.

What it does to your profit

Take the calculator's default example: a £20.00 item with £5.00 postage, £8.00 to make and £2.50 to post. For a seller who is not VAT registered:

VAT is added to the Offsite Ads fee as well, so the real cost is a little higher than 15% for sellers who cannot reclaim VAT. Here the advert sale earns £4.50 less. On the 12% rate the fee on a £25.00 order is £3.00 instead of £3.75.

Pricing for it

If you do not opt out, it is safest to check that you still make money on an advert sale. Using the pricing guide's candle example (materials, packaging, postage and £5.00 of your time), the break-even item price rises from £11.63 to £15.70 with a 15% Offsite Ads fee, and the price for a 30% margin rises from £19.85 to £30.88.
