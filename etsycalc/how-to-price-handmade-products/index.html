How to price handmade products on Etsy UK so you actually make a profit

Updated 3 November 2026. Figures checked against Etsy's own pages.

Most pricing mistakes on Etsy come from forgetting something: the 20p per order, postage you give away, VAT on Etsy's fees, or your own time. This guide builds a price from the bottom up so nothing is missed, then shows how to check it.

Quick answer. Add up every cost of one sale. Decide what profit you want, as a margin or an amount in pounds. Then solve for the item price that leaves that profit after Etsy's fees and VAT. The calculator's "Find the right price" panel does the last step for you.

Step 1: list every cost per sale

  • Materials for one item, including waste.
  • Packaging: box or mailer, tissue, labels, thank-you card.
  • Postage you pay to send it. Enter what you charge the buyer separately, because Etsy takes fees on that money.
  • Your time. Pay yourself something per hour. If you skip it the shop looks profitable but earns nothing for your work.
  • Anything else per item: gift extras, tools or equipment wear, if you count them.

Step 2: include Etsy's fees

For a seller who is not VAT registered, Etsy's fees on a standard sale come to about 13.2% of the order (item plus postage) plus roughly 42p, with no Offsite Ads. That is 6.5% transaction, 4% processing and 0.48% regulatory, with 20% VAT added, plus the 20p processing charge and the listing fee, also with VAT. A VAT-registered seller reclaiming VAT on fees pays about 11% plus 35p. See each fee explained.

Step 3: choose a target

  • Break-even is the lowest price that avoids a loss. Never go below it.
  • Profit in pounds is simple to reason about: "I want £5 left from each sale".
  • Profit margin is profit as a share of what the buyer pays. This calculator uses the total sale, item plus postage charged, which is the money Etsy handles.

Margin and markup are not the same. A 30% margin means profit is 30% of the sale price. A markup of 30% on cost gives a much lower margin.

Worked example: a hand-poured candle

Costs per candle: £4.20 materials, £0.80 packaging, £3.10 to post, and £5.00 for half an hour of your time at £10 an hour. The buyer pays £3.95 postage. No Offsite Ads, not VAT registered.

TargetItem price needed
Break-even (profit £0)£11.63
£5 profit per candle£17.39
20% margin£16.29
30% margin£19.85
40% margin£24.93

At £19.85 the candle sells for £23.80 including postage. Etsy's fees and VAT are £3.56, costs are £13.10, and £7.14 is left, which is 30% of the sale. Priced at £15 the same candle leaves £2.93 (15.5%). That is easy to do if you price by feel.

Check the awkward cases

  • Offsite Ads. If a sale comes from an Etsy-placed advert the fee is 15% or 12% of the whole order. For this candle the break-even price rises to £15.70 and the 30% margin price to £30.88. See the Offsite Ads guide.
  • VAT registration. If you reclaim VAT on Etsy's fees, the 30% margin price falls to £18.84. If you are registered and your prices include VAT, a sixth of every sale goes to HMRC and you need a higher price. See VAT on Etsy fees.
  • Free postage. You can fold postage into the price, but Etsy still takes its percentage fees on it, so the item price needs to cover that.
  • Discounts and sales. Run the numbers at the sale price, not the list price. A 20% off sale takes much more than 20% off your profit.

What margin should I aim for?

There is no universal answer. It depends on how much time the item takes, how much you want to reinvest, what buyers will pay, and whether you plan to use Etsy Ads. A sensible approach is to pick a margin that still leaves you paid for your time on a bad day, such as an Offsite Ads sale, and to check it with the calculator. Treat any general "ideal margin" figure as a starting point, not a rule.

Keep it up to date

Review prices when your material or postage costs change, when Etsy changes its fees, and when your sales cross a VAT or Offsite Ads threshold. The calculator can store your fee settings on your device so a re-check takes a minute.
